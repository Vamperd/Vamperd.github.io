## Dragon & Lion Dance

<div class="case-note case-note--team"><strong>Scene</strong> · Training and competing as part of a performance team.</div>

Dragon and lion dance gave me a physical understanding of collective rhythm. The performance only works when each person can hold their own role while listening closely enough to adjust to everyone else. Rehearsal made patience, repetition, and mutual trust feel practical rather than abstract.

<div class="story-photo-placeholder"><span>Authorized dragon and lion dance photographs will belong here.<br>Recommended: one rehearsal image and one competition image.</span></div>

The team received First Prize at the Chinese University Dragon and Lion Dance Championship in 2024 and 2025. More importantly, this experience continues to shape how I think about preparation: excellent group work is built long before the moment it becomes visible.

## Broadcasting

<div class="case-note case-note--team"><strong>Scene</strong> · Campus broadcasting work and live communication.</div>

Broadcasting is where preparation meets an audience. It has made me more attentive to pace, wording, and the responsibility of helping a room understand what matters in the moment. That practice transfers naturally to technical work: a useful idea still needs to be communicated clearly enough for others to act on it.

## RoboMaster Beyond the Robot

<div class="case-note case-note--team"><strong>Scene</strong> · The work around a competition robot that is not visible in a subsystem diagram.</div>

RoboMaster has taught me that a capable machine is only one part of a capable team. Preparation, training, communication, event support, and knowledge sharing are the work that gives technical effort continuity. These experiences complement my systems role by keeping the focus on how people coordinate under pressure.
